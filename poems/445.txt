Having submitted their tickets, families gather around the enclosure with an almost palpable excitement.
The stuff of fantasy, a beloved concept, the unicorn was suddenly discovered wandering the forests all around us and captivated the world’s attention.
Children yelped in glee, scientists were baffled at the introduction of magic, the common man wanted to witness the fabled creature with their own eyes.
Unicorns, in hordes, were captured and caged.
The well-meaning wildlife protection institutions provided their unicorns with vast grazing fields to thrive.
The majority were stored in for-profit zoos and street-side entertainment.
Some unicorns wore saddles and were ridden despite their spines and knees not being adapted to the heavy loads on their backs.
Some unicorns were trained to walk on their hind legs, shine magic out of their horn, or sing a shallow song.
Some unicorns were killed and gutted. Magic was extracted, processed, and refined. The culinary world was expanded with the introduction of unicorn venison steaks, tenderloin, backstrap, shoulder, and shank.
Some unicorns were merely used as a marketing tactic to attract new customers.
Weeks later and demand has only grown. The people can’t get enough of the novelty.
Merchandise was made. In stores, unicorn horns with the label “ethically sourced” could be purchased. Unicorn teeth and bones fashioned into beads and charms used in bracelets and necklaces. Unicorn leather jackets, hats, pants, shoes, belts, wallets, bags. Unicorn bone marrow and blood as alternative medicine.
The caged beasts of wonder, used for their wondrous appeal, grew thinner, grew weaker. Their ribs started to show, their coats started to become less gleaming. No one knew what they would eat, so they only offered what a horse or deer might.
Day after day, more humans to carry, more shows to perform, more tricks to learn, more wonder to share.
Harnesses jingle as the crack of whips echo through the air.
