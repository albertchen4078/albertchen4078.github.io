Though two sides of the same coin, love and hate, a dichotomy that has reverberated throughout the history of humanity across time and space, do not meet face-to-face.
Adjacent to fear, hatred is a form of survival that stems from a separation. If I hate something, I would stay away from it; if I truly hate something, then I would take the steps to permanently remove it. Hatred comes as an unwelcome knock on the door that sends shivers down your spine as you pick up your knife.
To love something, on the other hand, implies a form of attraction; a desire to spend time and resources for the sake of something else. Using a current day societal context, a true attraction does not end the moment two people come together. Instead, this love willingly persists. If time, wealth, and character are the only things that a person has to offer, then a lifelong love will have consumed it all.
Thus, unlike a repulsion (a hatred) that seeks to resolve conflicts quickly and, oft, messily, love is a considerably more exhausting endeavor.
The efforts in love are, effectively, rendered invisible due to the nature of love, but they remain nonetheless. So love and hate, though similar and intimate, have different gravities that make them like the sun and the moon.
A weak hatred is as free as a weak love; a strong hatred will rip your life from you; but all hatred is a lingering voice that we hope to drown out. To love is to give up your life; to love one more divides the life you spend with each, degrading the quality of your love until it becomes nothing but indifference if you could truly love the world and everything in it. Our love is finite and hate has its limits.
The way that things are, we shall be drawn by the waves of affection, pushed and pulled by hate and love, until we have found that we have been taken to the end where we have nothing left save for the memory of the life we spent.
